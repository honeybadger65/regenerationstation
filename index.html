The Living Practice System

Heart Learning · Weaving · Living Soil Practice · the Traveler's Journey

Living systems, not dead ones. Transformed, not transacted.

Purpose

The Living Practice System is an integrated model for Heart Learning, Weaving, and Living Soil Practice, linked by a shared exchange with regenerative travelers.

Its purpose is to let a village run all three strands, approve its own visitors, and train the next village without outside facilitators in the room.

Core principle

The community holds authority at every node. Each strand, visit, and dataset passes through community decision points before it moves on.

The exchange

Every visit is a two-way relationship. The visitor brings a skill, a question, or time spent learning, and receives the community's knowledge, craft, and care for the land in return.

Reciprocity is built into every exchange, and the transformation comes from what each person gives and receives, not from what is bought.

Team and roles

Community development lead

Owns listening circles, consent agreements, and the relationship with each village.

Learning designer

Keeps the six bricks coherent across home, school, and craft circle while letting each community adapt them.

Agroecology practitioner (Corretah's role)

Runs the Living Soil plots and sets soil-health indicators.

Craft economist

Designs fair pricing, sales channels, and how Weaving income flows back to the women.

MEL lead (Dr. Ringera or Dr. Paterra)

Holds the evidence base and records community-chosen measures.

Exchange steward

Manages visitor intake, pre-departure commitments, and the Rafiki Code in practice.

Governance and finance lead

Manages budgets, partner agreements, and the community's right to set limits.

Architecture

Community layer

Each village has a small council of mothers, craftswomen, teachers, and Corretah's soil group. The council sets priorities, approves visitors, and owns the local version of the six bricks.

Program layer

Heart Learning, Weaving, and Living Soil Practice run as parallel strands with shared calendars, shared materials, and one monthly joint session. Each strand reports to the council, not to headquarters.

Exchange layer

Visitors enter through the exchange steward, who confirms consent, skills offered, and the community's terms before any trip is scheduled.

Regenerative travel component

The traveler's journey runs in three phases, set out in the next section.

The Traveler's Journey

The traveler completes a preparation process with the exchange steward. They describe what they bring and what they hope to receive, read the Rafiki Code, and sign the pre-departure commitment.

They learn the community's terms: how many visitors are welcome, when, and what is off limits to share. A traveler seeking to consume experience is gently redirected. A traveler who wants to contribute is matched to a strand.

Regional network

Risks to design for

  • Visitor demand outpacing community capacity, which the council's power to limit intake addresses.
  • Craft income creating unequal benefit among women, which the craft economist monitors from the start.
  • Partner funding shaping priorities away from the community's own, which written agreements guard against.
  • Over-reliance on external facilitators, which the training cohort reduces over time.
